Problem

Poker can be a high-stakes game, and with high stakes comes high rewards. If you were able to consistently beat it, it could be used as a source of income (until the casino gets suspicious of your high earnings and bans you from the place anyways).

This is much easier said than done, there is still a large luck element to the game and you need to calculate the odds of each choice quickly and with limited information. While this is tricky for a human, an artificial intelligence agent might be more suited for this mathematical and probability task.

It’s not the first time AI has been used to win strategy games, one notable example would be the relationship between AI and Chess, going as far back as the 1900’s

In 1912, famous Spanish inventor Leonardo Torres Quevedo created an automaton that would play endgame Chess using an algorithm. With straight-forward logic, it would do moves that would eventually guarantee a checkmate. 

It was even able to detect illegal moves and comically refused to play if 3 illegal moves were made. Not the fastest or best, but it worked, and unlike other “robots” during that time, there was no human secretly operating it under the table, making it the first real AI Chess player.

Any AI history conversation should also mention Alan Turing. In 1948 they made a program called “Turochamp”, the first program meant to win a full game of Chess. It was not meant to be extremely smart, doing basic decisions, but the computers at the time could not handle the program due to its complexity.

Chess is a game with a hypothetical best move every game with no luck. So how can AI be used for Poker? Well, it should not be expected to win every game, it just has to win more than 50% of its games and try not to go bankrupt, this would make it profitable and statistically better than humans.
